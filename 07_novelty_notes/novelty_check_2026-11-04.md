# Novelty check, 2026-11-04

Searches run on 2026-11-04 (web search + Europe PMC REST; titles, authors, journals and DOIs copied from the records, not recalled from memory).
Question: has anyone (a) predicted the splicing response to knockdown of an RBP that was held out of training, using eCLIP binding as the perturbation descriptor; (b) inverse-mapped tissue splicing shifts onto an RBP-knockdown dictionary; (c) done either in human premalignant tissue?

## Verdict

Not done: (1) a benchmark in which the perturbed RBP is held out and described by its eCLIP binding at each event; (2) a measured ceiling (shRNA vs CRISPR, replicate-split) for splicing-level perturbation prediction, and the finding that TPM-derived PSI carries no reproducible knockdown signal while junction-level PSI does; (3) RBP-dictionary inverse mapping on human premalignant tissue.

Done, and must be cited and compared: SF activity inference from exon signatures with a perturbation dictionary (Anglada-Girotto 2026 Nat Commun; 2025 NAR) and KATMAP (2025 Nat Biotechnol). Our inverse mapping (Section 5) is therefore not a methodological contribution; it must (a) use VIPER/Anglada-Girotto networks as a baseline or as the engine, (b) add what they lack: predicted responses for RBPs outside the dictionary (from the forward model), eCLIP-anchored attribution, and human precancer cohorts.

Design consequences: Section 8.1 table updated; Section 5.1 to state that the dictionary approach follows Anglada-Girotto et al. and to include VIPER as a baseline; H3 retrieval test to report alongside their leave-one-out AUC.
